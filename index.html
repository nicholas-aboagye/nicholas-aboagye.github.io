Research

Water, land, and the gap between them

My work sits where hydrology meets land-use decision-making. The through-line is that water constraints are spatial before they are economic: they land on particular parcels, in particular years, under particular management regimes — and remote sensing can see that before a balance sheet can.

Thread A · Dissertation

Water security on working lands

A conceptual and geospatial framework for assessing water security across New Mexico and Texas working lands — availability, accessibility, quality, reliability and sustainability — and the environmental and human factors behind spatial differences in it. Then: how projected climate, hydrology and demand shift that picture, and which land uses are most exposed.

  • Extent: New Mexico & Texas
  • Unit: working lands
  • Output: suitability & adaptation
Thread B · Methods

Land cover change & classification

Twenty-five years of land cover change along the Northern Rio Grande Corridor, built on a multi-temporal Random Forest framework over Landsat imagery. The methodological contribution is in the comparisons: leaf-on against leaf-off against index-enhanced models, then an elevation-stratified post-classification composite that mosaics separate models across lowland and highland zones.

  • Series: 1995–2020
  • Sensor: Landsat
  • Split: 2,000 m elevation
Thread C · Applied

Hazards, energy & validation

Collaborative work on where land-use pressure and hazard meet: post-Hurricane Katrina coastal wetland change in Greater New Orleans, wildfire risk in Los Angeles and Ventura Counties, and the spatial determinants of wind energy siting in West Texas. A recurring concern here is honest validation — spatial block designs rather than random splits that leak across autocorrelated space.

  • Validation: spatial blocks
  • Domains: wetland, fire, wind
  • Scope: Gulf Coast, CA, TX
